Abstract
Step distillation reduces the cost of video generation, but reusing a LoRA trained for a longer trajectory can alter its functional effect or degrade target quality. Static parameter compatibility offers one perspective on this problem; our observations show that similar measured geometry can coexist with different adapter behavior under a shortened denoising schedule. We propose DART, a training-free method that combines low-rank coordinate transport with target-schedule response calibration using forward evaluations and no source training videos. On a four-step Wan2.2 target, DART-F improves the joint quality score from 0.9029 to 0.9227 and changes macro functional retention from

0.4644

Adapter-level results reveal positive functional effects for some adapters and strong attenuation with reduced negative functional effects for others. Evaluations on two additional targets show the same aggregate trend. These results motivate evaluating distilled-model LoRA reuse jointly through functional preservation and negative-transfer avoidance, without assuming recovery for every adapter.

Introduction
Reusing a LoRA trained on a base model for a few-step distilled model is a practical requirement for combining video generation speed with customization. Step distillation and consistency training reduce inference cost by shortening the denoising process
(
Salimans and Ho, 2022
;
Song et al., 2023
)
, while Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) enables lightweight adaptation while keeping the base-model parameters fixed
(
Hu et al., 2022
)
. As subject, style, and motion adapters accumulate, retraining these modules for every distilled version incurs repeated computation and may be impossible when private training data are unavailable. Few-step generation therefore requires a way to reuse existing adapters.
Direct reuse can cause an adapter to lose its intended effect or damage video structure. CASA relates transfer failure to spectral-routing interference between distillation and LoRA updates and coordinates them through spectral arbitration
(
Wang et al., 2026
)
, establishing the relevance of weight-space compatibility. We investigate a complementary issue: similar measured static compatibility can coexist with different adapter behavior under the target denoising schedule. This motivates measuring the adapter’s incremental response at target states and timesteps in addition to examining its parameter geometry.
Our central observation is that preserving base generation ability and preserving adapter function can be separate outcomes. A LoRA function arises from the joint action of its parameter update and the denoiser, and depends on the latent state, timestep, and conditioning input at which the update is applied. Distillation shortens the denoising process and changes these conditions. Consequently, a target model can maintain good base generation quality while producing a different response to the same LoRA update. We call the change in denoiser output before and after applying an adapter under the same input conditions the adapter’s incremental response. We use this response to guide reuse under target conditions, with functional preservation and avoidance of negative transfer as separate evaluation objectives. This perspective connects static parameter analysis to the adapter’s action during generation.
We study this separation in image-to-video generation. Four paired conditions load and unload the LoRA on the source and target models, allowing adapter-induced functional changes to be distinguished from the model’s own generation quality. Across the six retained adapters, static coordinate defects lie in a narrow range around
0.27
%
0.27\%
, yet functional transfer outcomes differ substantially; response diagnostics at target timesteps further show that source and target incremental responses can differ in both direction and magnitude (Section
3
). In the tested configurations, response calibration accounts for most of the quality gain, while its mean functional retention remains negative. The full configuration gives a further quality improvement and positive mean retention (Table
1
). These results indicate that mitigating quality damage from an adapter and recovering its intended function require separate evidence.
Based on these observations, we propose DART, a distillation-aware reparameterization method that guides LoRA reuse with incremental responses under the target denoising schedule. DART first constructs a low-rank distillation bridge and establishes transfer coordinates for the source update in clustered singular subspaces. It then uses paired forward evaluations to measure each channel’s response at target timesteps and solves for fixed channel coefficients that calibrate response direction, magnitude, and allocation across denoising steps. Coordinate transport supplies the channel directions to be calibrated, while response measurements determine how those directions should be combined in the target denoising process. For channel combinations that the selected probes cannot distinguish, calibration keeps the transported initial coefficients to limit changes unsupported by observations. The conversion requires neither source training videos nor backpropagation; the resulting adapter can be loaded directly into the target model.
We make the following contributions.
•
We identify a gap between measured static parameter compatibility and schedule-dependent adapter behavior, and use a four-anchor evaluation to separate target quality from adapter-induced functional effects.
•
We propose DART, a training-free reparameterization method that combines low-rank coordinate transport with forward-only response calibration under the target denoising schedule.
•
Across the primary Wan2.2 setting and two additional targets, DART improves aggregate quality and signed functional retention over Direct in the reported evaluations, while adapter-level analysis reveals both positive functional effects and mitigation of negative transfer.

Related Work
2.1
Parameter-efficient customization
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) represents a task update with two low-rank factors while keeping the base model fixed, following a broader line of parameter-efficient adapters and prompt-based updates
(
Houlsby et al., 2019
;
Li and Liang, 2021
;
Lester et al., 2021
;
Hu et al., 2022
)
. Video customization methods extend this lightweight interface to appearance, subject, and motion: Tune-A-Video and AnimateDiff adapt image diffusion models for video generation, while MotionDirector and VMC learn motion-specific controls
(
Wu et al., 2023
;
Guo et al., 2024
;
Zhao et al., 2024
;
Jeong et al., 2024
)
. These approaches learn or attach an adapter for a compatible model and denoising trajectory; DART addresses the complementary deployment problem of reusing that adapter after distillation changes the target coordinates and trajectory, without retraining.
2.2
Few-step video generation
Progressive distillation, consistency training, and fast ODE solvers shorten diffusion trajectories and reduce inference cost
(
Salimans and Ho, 2022
;
Song et al., 2023
;
Luo and others, 2024
;
Lu et al., 2022
)
. Video diffusion models extend these ideas to spatiotemporal generation, including Imagen Video, Make-A-Video, CogVideo, and ModelScopeT2V
(
Ho and others, 2022
;
Singer et al., 2022
;
Hong et al., 2022
;
Wang et al., 2023
;
Ho et al., 2022
)
, with Wan and HunyuanVideo providing representative open video foundation models
(
WanTeam et al., 2025
;
Kong et al., 2024
)
. VBench supplies broad quality and image-conditioning metrics for evaluating the resulting videos
(
Huang et al., 2024b
)
. These works improve the speed or measurement of generation, whereas DART studies whether a customization learned for a long trajectory remains functional on a shortened target schedule.
2.3
Data-free adapter transfer
Data-free adapter transfer avoids source training data. CASA provides a spectral-arbitration baseline for video LoRA reuse by relating failure to routing interference between distillation and LoRA updates
(
Wang et al., 2026
)
. Its weight-space analysis motivates examining parameter compatibility. Our observations raise an additional question: how does the update affect denoiser outputs at target states and timesteps? DART combines coordinate adaptation with target-schedule response calibration and evaluates quality separately from signed functional effects. We compare against CASA in its original training-free formulation. DART is an alternative transfer framework, rather than a modification of CASA; the comparison does not isolate the relative merits of the two coordinate-transport procedures.

Limitations.
The six-adapter evaluation has heterogeneous outcomes: three adapters lack positive primary retention, and one accounts for the largest quality gain. Four-anchor proxies measure selected effects rather than complete semantic preservation; near-zero source gains require exclusion. Robustness to probe count and repeated seeds remains unestablished. Conversion is target-specific, but its 96-minute model-pair preprocessing is reusable. Subtracting this setup gives DART-F a per-adapter cost of 130.5 minutes, with 528 forward evaluations. Its 79.91 GiB GPU memory figure is the maximum peak across eight conversion runs, not their mean (Appendix Table
15
). Larger adapter sets and repeated-seed evaluation remain future work.
6
Conclusion
LoRA reuse on distilled video models should be evaluated jointly through functional preservation and negative-transfer avoidance. In the measured adapter set, similar static geometry coexists with different functional outcomes. DART combines coordinate reparameterization with forward-only response calibration and improves aggregate quality and signed functional retention in the reported settings. Calibration contributes most of the quality gain, with an additional improvement from transport in the full configuration. Adapter-level results include positive functional effects and attenuation with reduced negative functional effects, motivating cautious evaluation of reuse behavior rather than an assumption of successful recovery for every adapter.

Appendix F
Extended Related Work and Positioning
DART studies reuse of an existing LoRA on a distilled image-to-video (I2V) model, conditioned on a reference image and a text prompt. The related literature addresses parameter compatibility, adapter representation, and functional alignment. These are overlapping perspectives: a mapping can support merging, and a weight update can be selected using response measurements. The distinction below concerns what each method transforms or matches, rather than assigning every method to a mutually exclusive category. Results from text-to-image generation or language modeling supply methodological context, not direct evidence of I2V transfer performance.
F.1
Weight-space LoRA transfer and merging
Weight-space methods address interference or compatibility between parameter updates. TIES-Merging trims small task-vector entries, resolves sign conflicts, and merges sign-consistent parameters
(
Yadav et al., 2023
)
. KnOTS specifically addresses LoRA merging by jointly aligning updates through SVD before applying a merging rule
(
Stoica et al., 2024
)
. These methods primarily combine capabilities from multiple adapted models. DART instead transfers one existing adapter to a distilled target; it does not aggregate a library of task adapters.
CASA is the closest evaluated baseline in the video reuse setting. It projects LoRA and model-drift updates into source singular coordinates and arbitrates routing interference within spectral clusters
(
Wang et al., 2026
)
. DART shares the concern for parameter compatibility, but uses transported rank-one channels as the directions for subsequent response calibration. Static compatibility is consequently an input to its construction, while measured incremental denoiser responses under the target schedule determine the channel coefficients. Our CASA comparison evaluates complete transfer procedures; it does not isolate which coordinate transformation is superior.
F.2
Adapter reparameterization and mapping
Reparameterization changes an adapter’s coordinates or factors, while mapping can connect different model representations. LoRA-X constrains the source adapter to a base-model subspace and transfers it to compatible target subspaces without additional transfer training
(
Farhadzadeh et al., 2025
)
. This is prior evidence that training-free cross-model transfer is possible; DART does not claim that idea itself. DART starts from an existing LoRA update and additionally measures how its transported channels act under the target denoising configuration. Preserving rank or spectral energy through a coordinate change does not establish preservation of the generated effect.
X-Adapter pursues plugin compatibility through trained feature-mapping layers between old and upgraded diffusion models
(
Ran et al., 2024
)
. It keeps the old model’s plugin interface and uses remapped features to guide the upgraded model. This differs from DART’s conversion into a target-loadable LoRA with fixed channel coefficients, without learning an additional feature-mapping network. The comparison concerns the representation and conversion procedure, not an unmeasured performance ranking.
F.3
Function-space alignment and response matching
Function-space methods use model behavior as the transfer signal. Knowledge distillation matches teacher outputs
(
Hinton et al., 2015
)
, while FitNets also uses intermediate teacher representations to guide a student
(
Romero et al., 2015
)
. Progressive distillation and consistency models connect related ideas to shorter diffusion trajectories
(
Salimans and Ho, 2022
;
Song et al., 2023
)
. DART operates after target distillation: its calibration descriptors concern the adapter-induced change relative to an unadapted baseline, rather than requiring the target to imitate the source model’s complete output. Its local measured-response model also does not guarantee equality of the final generated videos.
Trans-LoRA uses synthetic task data to train transferred adapters for new base models
(
Wang et al., 2024
)
. It illustrates why avoiding the original training dataset and avoiding further training are distinct properties. DART requires neither source training videos nor denoiser backpropagation during conversion, but it does use probe inputs and forward evaluations. Thus, source-data-free conversion should not be read as absence of calibration observations.
Gradient-free coefficient selection also has precedents. LoRAHub composes multiple task LoRAs using a few target-task examples and gradient-free optimization
(
Huang et al., 2024a
)
. DART instead fits coefficients for rank-one channels of one transported adapter using measured response descriptors across target timesteps and a regularized linear solve. The distinction lies in the coefficient granularity, measured signal, and deployment problem, rather than the absence of gradients alone.
F.4
DART’s specific combination and scope
DART combines coordinate transport with
target-schedule-conditioned, channel-level, forward-only calibration for distilled image-to-video LoRA reuse
. Each qualifier specifies a concrete part of the method:
•
Target-schedule-conditioned:
probes and target timesteps enter the measured response operator. Each I2V probe specifies a latent state, text prompt, and reference image, held fixed within a paired response measurement. Conversion produces one fixed coefficient vector for that configuration, not a separate runtime coefficient vector at every step.
•
Channel-level:
coefficients weight fixed rank-one directions within the transported update, rather than only scaling the complete adapter or weighting multiple task adapters.
•
Forward-only:
paired finite-difference evaluations at the bridge estimate channel responses, followed by the closed-form regularized solve.
Training-free
refers to the absence of iterative denoiser or LoRA training; calibration still fits coefficients and incurs forward-evaluation cost.
•
Distilled I2V LoRA reuse:
the converted adapter is deployed on the existing few-step image-conditioned target. The purpose is to recover useful adapter effects or reduce negative transfer while evaluating image-conditioning fidelity separately from functional retention. The reported evaluations do not establish transfer performance for text-to-video generation.
Table
16
summarizes these conceptual boundaries. Our positioning is the specific combination of these choices, not a claim that coordinate alignment, response matching, or gradient-free fitting is individually new. The reported component ablations support complementary effects in the evaluated setting; they do not establish superiority over every work discussed here or recovery for every adapter.
